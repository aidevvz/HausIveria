For buyers

Find your home in Vienna.

Buy an apartment or house in Vienna with an English-speaking agent. We guide international buyers through search, offer, contract and the land register.

Buying a home in Vienna should feel exciting, not overwhelming. If you are new to Austria, the process, the costs and the paperwork can be hard to read, especially in a second language. We represent your interests, explain each step in plain English, and make sure you know exactly what you are signing and what it will cost.

How we help you buy

We start by understanding what you actually want: the area, the type of home, the budget, and whether this is a place to live or an investment. Then we search, arrange viewings (in person or by video if you are abroad), and give you an honest opinion on each property, including what is wrong with it, not just what is good.

Our focus is the west and south-west of Vienna and the neighbouring part of Lower Austria. Once you have found the right home, we guide you through the offer, the contract and the handover.

The buying process in short

The usual path is a written offer (Kaufanbot), then a purchase contract drawn up and handled in escrow by a notary or lawyer, and finally registration of your ownership in the land register (Grundbuch). Along the way come the energy certificate, financing, and the incidental costs.

If you are buying from outside the EU, or simply want to understand the taxes and fees first, read our guide on buying property in Vienna as a foreigner.

What it costs to buy

Beyond the price, expect roughly 9 to 12 percent in incidental costs: property transfer tax of 3.5 percent, land register registration of 1.1 percent, the notary or lawyer who draws up and settles the contract, and the agent‘s commission where it applies. We lay out the full number for your specific purchase before you make any commitment.

Frequently asked questions

Good to know.

01Can foreigners buy property in Vienna?

Citizens of the EU, EEA and Switzerland buy on the same terms as Austrians. Buyers from outside the EU may need approval from the regional authority, which depends on the province and the type of property. We check this for your case before you commit. See our guide on buying as a foreigner.

02What extra costs come on top of the purchase price?

Typically around 9 to 12 percent: property transfer tax (3.5%), land register entry (1.1%), the contract and escrow handled by a notary or lawyer, and the agent's commission where it applies, plus VAT. We give you the full figure before you decide.

03Do you help buyers who are still abroad?

Yes. We regularly work with buyers who are relocating or investing from another country, with video viewings, English communication and support through the whole process.
