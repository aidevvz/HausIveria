Austria, and Vienna in particular, is a stable, attractive place to own property, and the city's large international community means many buyers are not Austrian. If you are buying from abroad or as a foreign resident, the good news is that it is very doable; you just need to understand the rules, the costs and the process before you commit. This guide gives you the overview. It is general information, not legal advice, so for your specific case get individual advice.

## Can foreigners buy property in Austria?

It depends on your citizenship:

- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** buy property in Austria on the same terms as Austrians. No special permit is required.
- **Buyers from outside the EU/EEA** may need approval from the regional land-transfer authority (Grundverkehrsbehörde) under the respective province's foreign-property-transfer rules (Ausländergrundverkehr). Whether approval is needed, and how it is handled, depends on the Bundesland and on the type of property.

Because these rules vary by province and change over time, the single most important step for a non-EU buyer is to clarify the requirement for your specific property before signing anything. We check this at the start, so there are no surprises later.

## What it costs: taxes and fees

Beyond the purchase price, budget for roughly **9 to 12 percent** in incidental costs (Kaufnebenkosten):

- **Property transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer):** 3.5 percent of the purchase price.
- **Land register entry (Grundbuch):** 1.1 percent for registering your ownership.
- **Contract and escrow (notary or lawyer):** roughly 1 to 3 percent plus VAT, for drawing up the purchase contract and handling the money securely in escrow.
- **Estate agent's commission:** up to 3 percent plus 20 percent VAT, where an agent is involved.

These percentages are stable and predictable, which makes the total cost easy to plan once you know the price.

## The buying process, step by step

1. **Search and viewings.** Define what you want, then view the shortlist, in person or by video if you are abroad.
2. **Offer (Kaufanbot).** A written offer sets out the price and key terms.
3. **Purchase contract (Kaufvertrag).** A notary or lawyer draws up the contract and acts as the escrow agent (Treuhänder), holding the funds until everything is in order.
4. **Energy certificate (Energieausweis).** The seller must provide one; its key values also appear in the listing.
5. **Land register (Grundbuch).** Your ownership is registered, which is the moment it becomes legally yours.

## Financing as a non-resident

Austrian banks do lend to non-residents, but usually expect a larger down payment and more paperwork than from a local buyer. If you need financing, clarify it early: knowing your realistic budget before you start viewing saves time and disappointment.
